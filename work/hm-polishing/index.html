Three decisions

Decision 01

Demonstrate, do not describe

Paint correction is invisible in a photograph and obvious in person. So the homepage does not open with a claim about quality — it opens with a panel of swirled paint and lets you take the swirls off yourself.

It is drawn on a canvas, so it is a few kilobytes and works on any phone. Nobody has to be told it is impressive.

Every shop on the street says "professional results". Only one of them hands you the polisher.

Decision 02

WhatsApp, because this is Dubai

A contact form would have been the default and the wrong answer. In the UAE the entire trade runs on WhatsApp, and the natural first message is a photograph of your own paint.

So the call to action is "send a photo of your paint", and it opens WhatsApp with the message already started. The shop answers with a price from a picture, the way that trade already works.

Decision 03

The site knows whether they are open

A bay like this keeps long hours and closes on Fridays. The site checks the clock in Dubai time and says whether it is open right now, or how long until it is — instead of printing a table of hours and making a customer do the arithmetic.

On purpose

The photo slots are empty, and they are not getting filled with stock

  • The site says, in its own words, that it is a record of the bay and not stock photography. Filling it with bought pictures of somebody else's workshop would break the only promise on the page.
  • Every photo slot is a single variable at the top of the file. For a shop that wants this site, dropping in their own photographs is a ten-minute job, not a rebuild.
  • Everything else works: the demo, the WhatsApp flow, the opening hours, the map, the copy.

Something
only you can do.

If your business has one thing a photograph cannot carry, that thing belongs on the homepage — working, not described.
