Four decisions

Everything else on the site follows from these.

Decision 01

Answer the price question before it is asked

Every enquiry started the same way: how much for my truck? The old page had no answer on it, so each one cost a phone call, and the ones who didn't feel like calling simply left.

So the site quotes. You pick your vehicle, the package, and a couple of add-ons, and a real price range appears — the same range the owner would have said on the phone. Then one button texts him with the details already written out.

The price is not hidden to protect a margin. It is hidden because nobody has ever built the shop a place to put it.

Decision 02

Text, not a contact form

Contact forms go to an inbox nobody with a pressure washer in their hands is checking. His customers already text him, so the site does the same thing they do.

Every call to action opens the phone's own messages app with the message pre-written: the vehicle, the package, the estimate. He answers between jobs, from the same thread he answers everyone else in.

Decision 03

His driveways, not stock photography

Every photo on the site is a car he actually detailed, in a Phoenix driveway, shot on his phone. A stock studio shot would have looked more expensive and sold less: the customer is trying to work out whether this is real, local and near them.

Decision 04

Build it so I can be fired

The site is one HTML file and a folder of photos. No page builder, no plugins, no login, no monthly software. He can open it in TextEdit and change a price himself.

It shipped with two plain-English documents: one on changing his own text, prices and photos, and one that walks him through pointing his domain at it, step by step, on his own accounts, with his own passwords. I never needed either.
